# Scanning your script with an iPhone

DramaDex reads a PDF. Your iPhone already has a document scanner that
makes one, so you do not need any other app. The whole thing takes about
a minute a page, and you only do it once per script.

## Before you start

- **Good light, no flash.** Daylight by a window or a bright lamp from
  the side. Flash puts a white glare spot on the page that eats words.
- **A flat page.** Open the book on a table and press the spine down.
  Hold the facing page back with your other hand or a mug. Curled
  edges turn straight lines into curves, and curved text reads badly.
- **Phone straight above the page.** Parallel to the table, not tilted.
  The scanner fixes a little skew, not a lot.
- **One printed page per picture.** Never a two-page spread. Two pages in
  one picture get read as one wide page and the lines interleave.
- **Keep the page number in the shot.** Everything downstream uses it.
- **Thumbs off the text, both margins.** A thumb holding the page down at
  the spine covers the first or last word of a line, and that word is gone
  for good. A thumb on the outer edge does the same to the other end of the
  line. Hold the page below the last line of print, or weigh it down with a
  mug or a card. On the first Communicating Doors scan this cost eight pages
  a re-shoot, and the re-shoot had to be read against the first photos
  because the thumb had moved to the other margin.
- **Short last lines are the easiest thing to lose.** A line that is just
  "we?" or "it—?" can be missed by the reader entirely. Nothing to do
  while shooting, but it is why the build checks line spacing afterwards.

## Scanning (Files app, saves straight to PDF)

1. Open **Files**. Go to **Browse**, then **On My iPhone** (or your
   iCloud Drive folder).
2. Tap the **⋯** button in the top right and choose **Scan Documents**.
3. Point the camera at the first page. In **Auto** mode it finds the
   page edges and snaps on its own when you hold still. If it keeps
   missing, tap **Auto** to switch to **Manual** and press the shutter
   yourself, then drag the corners onto the page edges.
4. After each page, the scanner shows a small thumbnail and goes
   straight back to the camera. Turn the page and keep going.
5. Got a bad one? Tap the thumbnail, then **Retake**. Do not delete and
   re-add at the end, or the page lands out of order.
6. Tap the **colour filter** (three circles icon) and pick
   **Grayscale**. Black & White is sharper but can drop faint print.
   Colour is fine too, just a bigger file.
7. When you reach the end of the act, tap **Save**. Name it like
   `communicating_doors_act1.pdf`.

**One PDF per act.** An act is thirty to fifty pages, which imports
quickly. A whole play in one file works but is slow on a phone and a
single bad page is harder to find.

Notes app works the same way (camera icon, **Scan Documents**), but you
then have to share the scan to Files to get a PDF. Files skips that
step.

## What to scan

**Everything, cover to cover.** Title page, cast list, the play, and the
furniture, property and effects plots at the back. The importer throws
away what it does not need, and the cast list and effects plot are
useful later. It is far easier to scan a page now than to find the book
again in week three of rehearsal.

## Importing

1. Open DramaDex and tap **Import a script**.
2. Pick **PDF** and choose the file from Files.
3. A scanned PDF says *This PDF is a scan — reading it page by page* and
   shows a progress bar. You can switch apps; it keeps working.
4. Confirm the cast when asked. Spaced-out names from the scanner
   (K R I S T I N) are already folded into their twin.
5. Anything the reader could not place is listed on the done screen.
   Those are the pages worth a second look.

## If a page reads badly

Rescan just that page in better light and save it as its own one-page
PDF. Tell whoever is building the play which printed page it replaces.
The printed page number is the name that makes this a one-step fix.

## Sending the files to someone else

Share the PDF from Files to **iCloud Drive**, **Google Drive**, **Mail**
or **Messages**, whichever you already use. AirDrop only reaches Apple
devices. Keep the file names as they are.
